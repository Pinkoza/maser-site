Fire-code alternative solution for a Calgary restaurant

A Calgary restaurant had PVC ceiling tiles installed throughout — and the City inspector would not accept the supplier's testing certificates as proof they met the Alberta Building Code. We were engaged to review the evidence and build the formal case for keeping the tiles.

Get a free quoteCall 778-803-5408

The situation

  • PVC ceiling tiles already installed across the restaurant's interior
  • The supplier maintained the tiles had been sold and installed across Ontario for over 20 years without issue, and provided testing certificates claiming they met safety requirements
  • The City inspector remained unsatisfied with the documentation — without accepted proof of the flame spread rating and smoke developed classification, the tiles could not stay
  • Ripping out a finished ceiling would have meant closing the dining room and redoing the fit-out

What we were engaged to do

  • Review the supplier's testing certificates and technical data sheets against the Alberta Building Code requirements for flame spread rating and smoke developed classification
  • Assess the installed assembly from site photographs — including whether mitigation measures such as fire-rated backing or sprinkler coverage could bring it into compliance
  • Prepare a formal opinion and alternative solution report that could be submitted to the City to justify retaining the tiles

How it ran

  1. ReviewThe inspector's notice, the supplier's certificates and the installation photos were reviewed against the Code text.
  2. AssessThe assembly was assessed as installed — not as a catalogue product — including the mitigation measures available.
  3. ReportA formal report set out the evidence and the compliance argument in the form the City could act on.
