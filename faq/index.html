Frequently asked questions

Permit drawings, stamping and permits in Canada · Maser Construction

Straight answers to the questions clients ask us most — what permit drawings are, what stamping means, what things cost and how the remote process works. For city-specific questions, see the areas we serve, or check the guides.

What are permit drawings, and do I need them?

Permit drawings are the technical drawings a city or municipality requires before it will issue a building permit for construction, renovation or a tenant improvement. They typically include a site plan, floor plans, elevations, sections, and code and zoning notes showing how the project complies with the building code. Most commercial fit-outs, restaurant and retail openings, and significant renovations cannot legally start construction without an approved permit, which means the drawings come first. Maser Construction prepares complete, permit-ready drawing sets for projects across Canada.

How much do permit drawings cost?

The cost depends on the size and complexity of the project, the type of space, how many disciplines are involved (architectural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing), and your municipality's requirements. Because every project is different, there is no honest one-size-fits-all price. Maser Construction starts every project with a free consultation, reviews the address and scope, and then gives you a clear fee before any work begins — so you know the full cost before you commit.

How long does it take to prepare permit drawings?

Timing depends on the project's complexity, how complete the starting information is, and whether stamping by a registered professional is required. A straightforward fit-out with good existing drawings moves faster than a complex project that needs a site survey or multiple design revisions. After the free consultation, Maser Construction gives you a clear schedule along with the fee, before work starts. City permit review time after submission is set by the municipality and is separate from drawing preparation time.

What is drawing stamping, and do my drawings need to be stamped?

Stamping means a registered professional engineer or architect has reviewed the drawings and applied their professional seal, taking responsibility for the design. Whether a stamp is required — and whose stamp — depends on the province, the municipality, the building type and the scope of work. Maser Construction arranges architectural and MEP stamping through registered professionals, and confirms the exact requirements for your province and project before quoting, so you are never sold a stamp you do not need or promised one that cannot be provided.

Which provinces and cities do you serve?

Maser Construction serves clients across Canada and works fully remotely, so your project location does not limit whether we can help. We prepare drawings and coordinate stamping for commercial, retail, restaurant and residential projects in all provinces. Because permit rules are municipal and stamping requirements are provincial, we confirm the specific requirements for your project's location at the start of every engagement.

I am opening a restaurant or retail store. What drawings do I need for a tenant improvement?

A tenant improvement usually requires a permit drawing set covering the new layout, along with mechanical, electrical and plumbing drawings for kitchen equipment, washrooms, HVAC and lighting changes. Your landlord and your city will typically both want to review the drawings, and some scopes require a registered professional's stamp. Maser Construction handles the full set — architectural plus MEP drafting and coordination — and supports the permit application, so your fit-out is not held up by missing or uncoordinated drawings.

What is a zoning check, and why does it matter before I sign a lease?

A zoning check confirms that your intended use — for example a restaurant, clinic or retail store — is actually permitted at that address under the municipality's zoning bylaw, along with basics like parking and signage rules. Signing a lease before checking zoning can leave you paying rent on a space you cannot legally use for your business. Maser Construction offers zoning checks as part of its permit support, so you can confirm the space works before you commit to it.

To start, we need the project address, a short description of what you want to build or change, and any drawings or documents you already have — for example an existing floor plan, a lease, or landlord requirements. If existing drawings do not exist, we will tell you what needs to be measured or surveyed. From there, the free consultation covers scope, the requirements for your municipality, and a clear fee and schedule before any work begins.

Everything is handled remotely: the consultation, document sharing, drawing preparation, stamping coordination and permit support all happen by phone and email, wherever in Canada your project is. You send the project details and any existing documents, we prepare the drawing set and coordinate any required stamping through registered professionals, and we support you through the permit application. You get a single point of contact — Nik, Project Manager — from the first call to the final set.

Do you also handle MEP drafting and coordination?

Yes. Maser Construction provides MEP drafting and coordination — mechanical (HVAC), electrical (power and lighting) and plumbing drawings — coordinated with the architectural set so the disciplines do not conflict on site. This can be part of a complete permit package or a standalone drafting service if you already have an architect or engineer of record. Coordinated MEP drawings reduce change orders and failed inspections during construction.

Can you help if my permit application gets comments or is refused?

Yes. Cities commonly issue comments asking for corrections or additional information, and responding with revised drawings is a normal part of the permit process. Maser Construction provides permit application support, including revising drawings to address municipal comments. If an application is refused on zoning grounds, a zoning check can clarify what is and is not permitted at the address before you decide on next steps.
